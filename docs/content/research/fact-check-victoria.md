## 1. Claims table

Verdict key: **TRUE** · **PARTLY** (true but needs a qualifier or correction) · **FALSE** · **COULD NOT VERIFY**

### Claim 1: Demolishing a building, in whole or in part, generally needs a building permit from a registered building surveyor.

**Verdict: TRUE.** Add the exemptions qualifier.

- All building work needs a building permit unless the Building Regulations 2018 (Schedule 3) exempt it. Building work includes demolition.
- A private or municipal *registered* building surveyor issues the permit.
- Common exemption: demolishing a freestanding Class 10 building (shed, garage, carport, fence, etc.) that is:
  - not masonry
  - 40 m² or less
  - not a risk to public safety
  - not on the Heritage Register.
- The BPC lists "some minor alterations or demolitions" as possible exemptions.

**Safe wording:** "In Victoria, demolishing all or part of a building usually needs a building permit from a registered building surveyor. Some minor work is exempt, such as a small non-masonry shed. We'll tell you early whether your job needs a permit and can coordinate it."

**Sources:**
- https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/building-surveyors/permits/when-is-a-building-permit-required
- https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0018/21474/Building-BP-01-When-is-a-building-permit-required-CURRENT-20-Dec-2023.pdf (BP 01)
- https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/home-owners/home-renovation-and-building-essentials/bushfire-protection/rebuilding-after-a-bushfire ("A building permit is required before you can demolish a building or part of a building…")

### Claim 2: Council "report and consent" (section 29A, Building Act 1993): what it covers and how long council has.

**Verdict: PARTLY.** The common shorthand ("heritage, or more than half the volume") is incomplete.

Section 29A report and consent from the responsible authority (usually the council) is needed when **either** test is met.

1. **Volume test.** The proposed demolition, plus any demolition done or permitted in the previous **3 years**, would remove **more than half the volume** of the building.
2. **Facade test.** The work removes **any part of a street-facing facade** that is at least partly visible from that street.
   - Facade includes external walls, verandas, balconies, part of a roof, and a chimney.
   - Lanes, rights of way and driveways are not "streets".
   - On a corner block, both facades count.
   - Being partly hidden by fences or trees does not exempt it.

Other points:

- **Heritage is a separate trigger.**
  - A heritage overlay usually means a **planning permit** is needed for demolition. If that permit hasn't been obtained, council *must* refuse consent.
  - Places on the **Victorian Heritage Register** also need the Executive Director (Heritage Victoria)'s report and consent under s28(4).
- **Time to respond:** council has **15 business days** (Building Regulations 2018, reg 34).
- **If council doesn't respond in time,** consent is deemed given. The exception is when a required planning permit hasn't been obtained; then consent is deemed **refused**.
- **Fee:** capped at 5.75 fee units (reg 36). Don't publish this.
- **Consent validity:** one council said 12 months. I could not verify this from an official state source, so **don't publish it**.

**Safe wording:** "If the work removes more than half the building, counting demolition over the past three years, or removes any part of a street-facing facade, the council must give 'report and consent' before the demolition permit is issued. Councils have 15 business days to respond. Heritage overlays usually also need a planning permit."

**Source:** https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0016/21517/Building-DE-01-Demolition-of-buildings_CURRENT-13-May-2024.pdf (DE 01, current, 13 May 2024; landing page https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/resource-hub/practice-notes/de-01-demolition-of-buildings-report-and-consent)

### Claim 3: Removing an internal non-load-bearing wall, or a kitchen or bathroom strip-out, may not need a building permit.

**Verdict: TRUE as "may not".** Never write "doesn't need".

Schedule 3 exempts **alterations** only if the work:

- does not adversely affect structural soundness
- does not change floor area or height
- does not involve underpinning or replacing footings
- does not remove or alter **any element contributing to the support of another element**
- does not project beyond the street alignment
- does not adversely affect public or occupant safety
- is not on a Heritage Register building
- does not relate to or affect an **essential safety measure**, such as fire safety items.

The repair and renewal exemption covers like-for-like work. Examples: "retiling a bathroom or kitchen" and "repairing or renewing non-fire rated wall, ceiling or floor linings".

**A permit IS needed for:**

- removing or altering load-bearing or supporting elements
- footing or underpinning work
- changing floor area or height
- work affecting essential safety measures
- work on heritage-registered buildings.

Bracing is a trap: a wall that carries no roof load may still be a **bracing wall**. The BPC's earthquake guide warns about removing internal bracing walls without replacing the bracing (*search index*).

**Safe wording:** "Taking out a non-structural internal wall or stripping a kitchen or bathroom often doesn't need a building permit. A permit is needed if the work removes anything that supports or braces the building, changes the floor area, or affects fire-safety features. If we're not sure a wall is structural, we get it checked before we touch it."

**Sources:**
- BP 01 (above), sections "Repair, renewal, or maintenance" and "Alterations to a building"
- https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/resource-hub/safety-guides/earthquake-design-and-construction (*search index*)

### Claim 4: Removing a load-bearing wall needs an engineer and a building permit.

**Verdict: PARTLY.** The permit part is TRUE. The engineer part is standard practice rather than a single quoted rule.

- **Permit:** removing an element that contributes to the support of another element takes the work outside the exemption, so a permit is needed (BP 01).
- **Structural stability:** for partial demolition, the permit application must include "computations or other information" showing the remaining building will stay structurally stable (reg 26, as summarised in DE 02).
- **Engineers:** the BPC expects demolishers to consult "endorsed building engineers" on demolition works and on propping.

**Safe wording:** "Removing a load-bearing wall needs a building permit. In practice, the building surveyor will want structural details, usually from a registered engineer, showing how the load is carried, such as a new beam. We can coordinate the engineer and the permit."

**Sources:**
- BP 01
- DE 02 (under review)
- https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/resource-hub/guides/what-is-demolition-work

### Claim 5: Electricity, gas, water and sewer must be disconnected or abolished before demolition.

**Verdict: TRUE.** Make one correction: say **"abolished"**, not "disconnected", for power and gas.

**Electricity (Energy Safe Victoria):**

- Supply must be **abolished**, meaning the meter and supply cable are permanently removed, before demolition. This covers overhead *and* underground supply.
- A "disconnection" (switched off) still leaves a physical connection to the network, and ESV says it is **not safe** to start demolition.
- The property owner, developer, builder **and demolition business** all have responsibility.

ESV's six steps:

1. Settle the account.
2. Submit the VESI Application for Abolishment of Electrical Supply.
3. Tell the builder and demolisher.
4. Give the crew clear access.
5. **Visually confirm the meter has been removed.**
6. Demolish.

Other electricity points:

- For underground supply, the distributor abolishes it at the pit or pillar, and your electrician then tests and removes the underground mains.
- Abolishment is "usually free" (ESV).
- Powercor says a standard overhead abolishment can take up to four weeks from when the property is vacant.
- Network assets (poles, pillars, underground cables, substations) need a separate request and may cost money.

**Gas:**

- Ask your **gas retailer** to raise an abolishment.
- The distributor removes the meter, then cuts and caps the service line, usually in the street.
- AusNet: meter removal takes about 2 business days, and the service disconnection takes up to 15 business days.
- Multinet: you can apply directly only if there is no active retailer account or the property is vacant.

**Water and sewer:**

- Apply to the water corporation.
- Yarra Valley Water:
  - Only YVW's metering contractor can remove a meter.
  - Sewer disconnection needs online approval. Then either a licensed plumber caps the private drain, or an accredited Live Sewer Contractor cuts and seals the connection.
- South East Water: apply through PropertyConnect ("Sewer – Cut & Seal" and "Water – Remove my water service").
- Greater Western Water: not checked; follow the same pattern.

**Safe wording:** "Before demolition, power and gas must be abolished, not just switched off, and water and sewer disconnected through your water corporation. We'll tell you what to book and when, and we won't start until the meters are physically gone."

**Sources:**
- https://www.energysafe.vic.gov.au/community-safety/working-tradespeople/disconnection-and-abolishment-electricity-supply (reviewed 8 Sep 2026)
- https://www.powercor.com.au/services/connecting-to-the-electricity-network/abolish-an-electricity-supply
- https://www.ausnetservices.com.au/gas/connections/requesting-a-gas-disconnection-reconnection-or-abolishment
- https://www.multinetgas.com.au/abolishment-and-disconnection-requests

### Claim 6: Asbestos licensing rules and the register or survey requirement.

#### 6a. "Buildings built before 1990 may contain asbestos."

**Verdict: TRUE.**

- The Victorian Asbestos Hub: "If your home was built or renovated before 1990, it likely contains asbestos."
- WorkSafe: asbestos was used until the mid-to-late 1980s. The total ban came in on 31 Dec 2003, so "buildings and products from before 2004 may still contain asbestos".

**Safe wording:** "If your home was built or renovated before 1990, it probably contains asbestos. Anything built before 2004 can still contain it."

#### 6b. "Non-friable asbestos over 10 m² needs a Class A or B licensed removalist."

**Verdict: PARTLY.** The real trigger is **more than 10 m² OR more than 1 hour of removal work in any 7-day period**. Either one requires a licence.

**Safe wording:** "Removing more than 10 m² of bonded (non-friable) asbestos, or more than an hour's removal work in a week, needs a WorkSafe Class A or Class B licensed removalist."

#### 6c. "Friable asbestos needs Class A."

**Verdict: TRUE.** Any amount of friable asbestos needs a Class A licence. A Class B holder cannot remove friable asbestos.

#### 6d. "Under 10 m², a non-licensed person can remove it."

**Verdict: PARTLY.**

- An employer or self-employed person without a licence may remove **non-friable** asbestos only if it is **10 m² or less AND no more than 1 hour in total in 7 days**. The hour is shared across all their workers: 2 people get 30 minutes each.
- The full safety rules still apply: wetting, PPE, decontamination, disposal, signs.
- A homeowner removing it from their own home is not covered by the OHS Regulations, but the Hub **recommends a licensed removalist for any amount**.

**Safe wording:** "Very small amounts of bonded asbestos can legally be removed without a licence in limited cases. We recommend, as the state's asbestos hub does, using a licensed removalist for any amount."

#### 6e. "An asbestos register or survey is required before demolition or refurbishment of pre-2003 buildings."

**Verdict: FALSE as worded for Victoria.**

The "built before 31 Dec 2003" register cut-off comes from the *model WHS Regulations* used in other states. Victoria's OHS Regulations 2017 (Part 4.4) have **no build-date cut-off**. The actual rules are:

- **Workplaces:** the person who manages or controls the workplace must identify asbestos and keep an asbestos register. The register is reviewed before demolition or refurbishment and at least every 5 years. If there is no register, "a determination of the presence of any asbestos… must be done before the demolition or refurbishment work commences."
- **Homes:** a home has no register. Once a contractor is engaged to demolish or refurbish, it becomes **their** workplace. They must identify asbestos likely to be disturbed, or engage a competent person to do so. They must also ensure it is removed, so far as reasonably practicable, **before demolition starts** (OHS Regs r244(5)). Partial demolition is allowed to gain access to asbestos.
- **Building permits:** the Hub says "Building surveyors require an asbestos survey report before issuing a building permit for demolition."
- **Notification:** licensed removalists must notify WorkSafe:
  - at least 5 days before work in most cases
  - at least 24 hours before for 10 m² or less of non-friable asbestos
  - within 24 hours in unexpected situations.
- **Clearance:** after removal, an independent inspection and clearance certificate are required.
- **Asbestos installed after 1 Jan 2004** must be notified to WorkSafe, under the Dangerous Goods Act 1985. This does not apply to homes used only as homes.

**Safe wording:** "Before any demolition or strip-out, asbestos likely to be disturbed has to be identified and, where practicable, removed first. On homes, that's the contractor's legal job. We arrange a survey, use a licensed removalist and get a clearance certificate before demolition starts."

- https://www.asbestos.vic.gov.au/permits-notifications-compliance/
- https://www.asbestos.vic.gov.au/how-to-identify-asbestos-safely/

### Claim 7: Asbestos waste must go to an EPA-licensed facility and be transported under EPA rules.

**Verdict: TRUE.** Tighten the wording: EPA says "a **place authorised to receive it**", which in practice means a landfill on EPA's authorised list.

**Business or trade asbestos** (commercial, industrial or trade activity) is **reportable priority waste**:

- the transporter needs an EPA permission (A10b, or A10a for high-risk loads)
- every movement must be logged in **Waste Tracker**
- you must confirm the landfill accepts industrial asbestos before transport.

**Householders** taking their own household asbestos in their own vehicle:

- don't need Waste Tracker or a permission
- must package it correctly and use a domestic-accepting landfill
- should call ahead, because sites only accept small amounts and some sites are "commercial only" or "domestic only".

**Never** put asbestos in skips, household bins or hard rubbish, and never bury it.

**Metro sites on EPA's list** (reviewed June 2026; call ahead):

- Bulla (Australian Recycling Corporation)
- Wollert (Hanson / Heidelberg Materials)
- Dandenong South (Veolia, commercial only)
- Fingal (Mornington Peninsula, domestic only)

**Safe wording:** "Asbestos goes only to an EPA-authorised landfill, carried by a transporter with EPA permission and tracked in EPA's Waste Tracker. We give you the Waste Tracker record as proof."

**Sources:**
- https://www.epa.vic.gov.au/dispose-asbestos-waste (list updated 23 Sep 2026)
- https://www.epa.vic.gov.au/manage-asbestos-waste (updated 11 Mar 2026)
- https://www.asbestos.vic.gov.au/disposal/

### Claim 8: When protection work notices are needed for demolition near a boundary.

**Verdict: TRUE as a concept.** The trigger is the **building surveyor's decision**, not a fixed distance.

- When you apply for a permit, the building surveyor decides whether protection work is needed to protect "adjoining property".
  - Protection work includes underpinning, shoring, overhead protection, and other work keeping the neighbour's property stable.
  - Adjoining property can include streets, lanes, footpaths and rights of way.
- If it is needed:
  - The owner serves a **protection work notice** on the adjoining owner.
  - The neighbour has **14 days** to respond. No response counts as agreement.
  - If the neighbour disagrees, the building surveyor makes a determination. Either party can appeal to the Building Appeals Board within 14 days.
  - The owner must hold **insurance** covering the adjoining property for 12 months after completion. A standard construction or public liability policy "will not normally" be enough.
  - The owner and neighbour must agree a **condition survey** (record of existing cracks) before work starts.
  - The owner must pay the neighbour's reasonable costs, such as their engineer's fees.
- **The permit cannot be issued until the protection work process is complete.**

**Safe wording:** "If your demolition is close to a boundary, the building surveyor may decide protection work is needed, such as propping or overhead protection. Your neighbour then gets a formal notice and 14 days to respond, and the permit can't be issued until that's done. We'll flag this early so it doesn't hold up your start date."

**Sources:**
- https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/home-owners/home-renovation-and-building-essentials/protecting-adjoining-properties (updated 30 Jun 2026)
- DE 02: no part of an external wall on or within 3 m of a street alignment may be pulled down except during hours the building surveyor directs.

### Claim 9: Before You Dig (BYDA) should be checked before excavation or slab removal.

**Verdict: TRUE.**

- BYDA enquiries are **free**. Lodge at least **one business day** before work starts.
- Plans show the networks' assets, but **not privately installed services** on the property. BYDA recommends a professional utility locator.
- You may not be insured for damage if you didn't lodge an enquiry.
- Energy Safe Victoria's abolishment page also tells people to use BYDA before digging.
- Slab and footing removal is ground-disturbing work, so the advice applies. BYDA itself speaks of "excavation" and "digging".

**Safe wording:** "Before we break out slabs, footings or dig, we lodge a free Before You Dig Australia enquiry and, where needed, use a locator for private services the plans don't show."

### Claim 10: E-waste landfill ban, items banned from general rubbish, and waste levy context.

**Verdict: TRUE**, with notes below.

**E-waste:**

- It has been illegal to put e-waste in household rubbish or landfill since **1 July 2019**.
- E-waste is anything with a plug, battery or power cord.
- EPA: "Landfills in Victoria are not able to receive e-waste."
- Batteries: never in household bins (lithium-ion fire risk).

**Not for landfill or general bins:**

- EPA: anything toxic, corrosive, or that will burn or explode. Examples: e-waste and batteries, fluorescent bulbs, mercury switches, motor oil, paints, thinners, varnishes. These go to hazardous-waste drop-off.
- Asbestos: never in general, recycling or green bins.
- Gas bottles and tyres: council lists exclude them from kerbside and hard rubbish (Glen Eira, Monash, Darebin). EPA's household page doesn't name them.

**Demolition and renovation waste:**

- "Most construction and demolition waste is industrial waste."
- It must go to a lawful place, including waste in skips or taken by a contractor.
- **The homeowner remains responsible** for where a contractor or skip company takes it, and can be fined for using an illegal dumper.

**Levy:**

- A waste levy is paid on all waste received at landfill. The metro rate rose on 1 July 2026.
- Do not publish figures; say "landfill levies".

**Safe wording:** "We sort strip-out and demolition waste so recyclables are recycled. E-waste, batteries, chemicals and asbestos never go to landfill. Everything goes to licensed facilities, and we keep the tip dockets."

**Sources:**

### Claim 12: Do demolition or strip-out contractors need a specific licence or registration?

**Verdict: TRUE.** Registration is required for demolition work. Strip-out depends on the job.

**BPC Demolisher classes:**

| Class | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Low rise** | Whole or part dismantling up to 2 storeys, excluding special buildings. **Also stripping of internal fittings, fixtures, services and non-load-bearing elements of *unoccupied* buildings** up to 2 storeys. |
| **Medium rise** | The same, up to 5 storeys. |
| **Unlimited** | All buildings, including special buildings: precast or tilt-up panel, prestressed concrete, towers, chimneys and similar. |

**Who can be the builder on a demolition permit:** the builder named on the permit should be registered as a Demolisher in the right class, or as a **Domestic Builder (Unlimited)** for homes (DE 02).

**Owner-builders:**
- An owner-builder **cannot** carry out demolition or subfloor work (BPC owner-builder guide).
- The BPC (as VBA) won't issue an owner-builder certificate of consent for demolition (DE 02).

**Cost threshold: BPC pages conflict.**
- "Some types of work, such as **demolitions**, electrical work and structural work must be done by a registered practitioner **regardless of the cost**" (BPC, updated 5 Nov 2026).
- The bushfire page says a licensed practitioner is needed "if the cost of works exceeds $10,000".
- Treat demolition as needing registration at any value.

**Strip-out:**
- A small single-trade strip-out with no permit may not require registration.
- Strip-out of unoccupied buildings sits inside the Demolisher scope.
- If the strip-out is domestic building work over $10,000 involving more than one trade, a registered builder and a major domestic building contract apply.

**Separate licences still apply:**
- WorkSafe asbestos removal licence (Class A or B)
- Licensed or registered plumbers and electricians for services work
- WorkSafe high-risk work licences for plant (*not checked here*).

**Insurance:** Demolisher (low and medium rise) registrations need professional indemnity cover of at least **$5,000,000** (BPC insurance page, *search index*).

**Safe wording:** "Demolition in Victoria has to be done by a builder registered with the Building and Plumbing Commission. Check our registration at bpc.vic.gov.au/check. Asbestos removal is done under a separate WorkSafe licence."

**Use only if true:** state the actual class and number, e.g. "registered Demolisher (Low rise), DB-L…". **Do not** write "VBA-licensed".

## 2. Homeowner questions and answers (40–60 words each)

Most official sites publish guidance pages rather than formal FAQ lists. These questions follow the headings those official pages answer. No legal advice, no prices.

**1. Do I need a permit to demolish part of my house?**
Usually, yes. Victorian law treats demolition as building work, so demolishing all or part of a building generally needs a building permit from a registered building surveyor. Some minor demolition is exempt, such as a small freestanding non-masonry shed of 40 m² or less. A building surveyor can confirm whether your job needs one.
*Source:* https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/building-surveyors/permits/when-is-a-building-permit-required

**2. Do I need a permit to remove an internal wall or strip out a kitchen or bathroom?**
Not always. Non-structural alterations, like retiling or replacing linings, can be exempt. A permit is needed if the work removes or alters anything that supports another part of the building, affects structural soundness, changes floor area or height, touches fire-safety features or involves a heritage-registered building. Some non-load-bearing walls still brace the house, so get it checked.
*Source:* https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0018/21474/Building-BP-01-When-is-a-building-permit-required-CURRENT-20-Dec-2023.pdf

**3. Does the council have to consent to my demolition?**
Sometimes. Before a demolition permit is issued, the building surveyor must get council "report and consent" if the work, with demolition in the past three years, removes more than half the building's volume, or removes any part of a street-facing facade that can be seen from the street. Council has 15 business days to respond.
*Source:* https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/resource-hub/practice-notes/de-01-demolition-of-buildings-report-and-consent

**4. My house was built before 1990. Does it have asbestos?**
It may well. The Victorian Asbestos Hub says homes built or renovated before 1990 are likely to contain asbestos, often in wet-area wall sheeting, eaves, vinyl floor tiles and cement sheet. You can't tell by looking: only a NATA-accredited laboratory can confirm it. Before renovating or demolishing, get an asbestos survey of the areas affected.
*Source:* https://www.asbestos.vic.gov.au/homeowner-or-renovator/ and https://www.asbestos.vic.gov.au/how-to-identify-asbestos-safely/

**5. Can I remove asbestos myself?**
Victorian law lets homeowners remove very small amounts in their own home in limited circumstances, but the Victorian Asbestos Hub recommends a licensed removalist for any amount. Once you hire someone, your home becomes their workplace: they need a WorkSafe licence for any friable asbestos, or for non-friable work over 10 m² or one hour in seven days.
*Source:* https://www.asbestos.vic.gov.au/safe-removal-steps/ and https://www.worksafe.vic.gov.au/understand-and-manage-asbestos-workplaces

**6. Who is responsible for finding asbestos before my renovation or demolition?**
When you engage a contractor to demolish or refurbish your home, it becomes their workplace. Under Victoria's OHS Regulations, they must identify asbestos likely to be disturbed, or engage a competent person to do it, and ensure it is removed, so far as reasonably practicable, before demolition starts. Ask how they'll do this.
*Source:* https://www.worksafe.vic.gov.au/understand-and-manage-asbestos-workplaces and https://www.asbestos.vic.gov.au/builder-or-trades/

**7. Where does asbestos waste go, and how do I know it was disposed of properly?**
Asbestos must go to a landfill authorised by EPA Victoria to accept it, never a skip, household bin or hard rubbish. Asbestos from commercial or trade work is reportable priority waste: the transporter needs EPA permission and must record the load in Waste Tracker. Ask your removalist for the Waste Tracker record.
*Source:* https://www.epa.vic.gov.au/manage-asbestos-waste and https://www.asbestos.vic.gov.au/disposal/

**8. Can I put renovation or demolition waste out for council hard rubbish?**
Usually not. Melbourne councils' hard rubbish services generally exclude building, renovation and demolition waste such as bricks, concrete, plasterboard, tiles and rubble, plus asbestos, gas bottles and chemicals. Rules vary: Glen Eira also refuses toilets, baths and cabinetry, while Darebin accepts some bathroom fittings. Check your council's list before booking.
*Source:* https://www.gleneira.vic.gov.au/services/rubbish-and-recycling/hard-rubbish-branches-and-cardboard and https://www.darebin.vic.gov.au/Waste-environment-and-climate/Hard-waste/Our-free-hard-waste-collections

**9. How do I get the power and gas disconnected before demolition?**
Switching off isn't enough. Energy Safe Victoria says electricity must be abolished, meaning the distributor permanently removes the meter and supply cable, overhead and underground, before demolition. Settle your account and apply through your distributor. For gas, ask your retailer to arrange an abolishment, where the distributor removes the meter and cuts and caps the service.
*Source:* https://www.energysafe.vic.gov.au/community-safety/working-tradespeople/disconnection-and-abolishment-electricity-supply and https://www.ausnetservices.com.au/gas/connections/requesting-a-gas-disconnection-reconnection-or-abolishment

**10. Do I have to notify or protect my neighbours?**
If your work is close to a boundary, the building surveyor decides whether protection work, such as underpinning, shoring or overhead protection, is needed. If it is, you must serve a protection work notice on the neighbour, who has 14 days to respond; silence counts as agreement. The permit can't issue until this process is complete.
*Source:* https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/home-owners/home-renovation-and-building-essentials/protecting-adjoining-properties

### Reserve questions

**11. What if my property has a heritage overlay?**
A heritage overlay generally means you need a planning permit from council before demolishing all or part of the building, and the building permit cannot be issued until you have it. If the place is on the Victorian Heritage Register, Heritage Victoria's consent is also needed. Check with your council's planning department early.
*Source:* DE 01 (above)

**12. What about the water meter and sewer?**
Contact your water corporation first. Yarra Valley Water, for example, says only its metering contractor can remove a water meter, and sewer disconnection needs online approval, then a licensed plumber to cap the private drain or an accredited live sewer contractor to cut and seal the connection. South East Water uses similar PropertyConnect applications.
*Source:* https://www.yvw.com.au/help-advice/develop-build/home-owners/knock-down-and-rebuild and https://southeastwater.com.au/building-and-development/home-owners/building/

**13. Can I do the demolition myself as an owner-builder?**
No. The BPC's owner-builder guide says an owner-builder cannot undertake demolition or subfloor work, and the BPC will not issue an owner-builder certificate of consent for demolition because of its high risk. Demolition requiring a permit should be done by a registered practitioner, such as a BPC-registered Demolisher or Domestic Builder (Unlimited).
*Source:* https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/_media/resource-hub/guides/PRS-HAN-008-Owner-Builder-Information-and-Study-Guide.pdf and DE 02

## 3. Commonly gotten wrong (flag in copy review)

1. **"VBA-registered" or "VBA-licensed".** The regulator has been the **BPC** since 1 July 2025.
2. **"Report and consent only applies to heritage or over 50%."** Removing **any visible part of a street-facing facade** also triggers it. Heritage overlays trigger a **planning permit**, not 29A by themselves.
3. **"Council has 10 days" or "15 days".** It is **15 business days**. If council is silent, consent is deemed given, unless a required planning permit is missing.
4. **"An asbestos register is required for pre-2003 buildings."** That is the model WHS rule used in other states. Victoria has **no date cut-off**. Homes have no register; the **contractor** must identify asbestos before demolition or refurbishment.
5. **"Under 10 m², anyone can remove it."** The limit is **10 m² AND 1 hour per 7 days**, non-friable only, and full safety controls still apply.
6. **"Power disconnected" or "gas disconnected".** It must be **abolished**. A disconnection still leaves a live connection to the network.
7. **"Non-load-bearing wall means no permit."** Bracing walls and fire-safety elements can still need a permit. Say "may not need".
8. **"Hard rubbish will take your strip-out waste."** Mostly false, and rules vary by council.
9. **Asbestos "to an EPA-licensed tip".** The precise wording is "a place **authorised** by EPA to receive it". For trade jobs, add **Waste Tracker**.
10. **"Strip-out doesn't need a registered builder."** It depends on the job. Demolition needs registration regardless of cost (BPC). A Demolisher's scope includes strip-out of unoccupied buildings.
