## Step 4 — synthesis

### Common section pattern
Hero (H1 with "Melbourne" + rubbish removal, one-line promise, quote + call CTAs) → quote form or availability check → trust bar (rating, years, insurance/NDIS) → services/job types grid → how it works (3–5 steps) → pricing or "upfront pricing" explainer → recycling/sustainability → reviews → service areas/suburbs → FAQ → closing CTA. Big brands add suburb link farms; locals add awards and repeated forms.

### Gaps none of them fill well
1. **Heavy materials, honestly priced.** 1800-GOT-JUNK? caps concrete at "one foot" per truck, Junk.com.au excludes prescribed soil and says nothing about bricks/concrete, Easy says nothing, Melbourne Junk Removal refuses large quantities. Only Demo Bros says "by weight", and even it gives no number or method. Nobody explains *why* heavy loads cost more (tip fees by tonne, truck weight limits) or what a typical brick/concrete load costs.
2. **Unit translation.** Competitors mix m³, truck fractions and "trailer loads". No one says what their trailer or truck holds in m³, so a buyer cannot compare a $290 "half truck" with $99/m³. Quote Yard tells buyers to ask exactly this.
3. **Council hard rubbish vs paid removal, by real limits.** Only Junk.com.au addresses it, and it links away. Nobody says "your council gives you 1–4 m³ a year, excludes X, and you have to drag it to the kerb; here's when that's enough and when it isn't."
4. **Photo quote on the landing page.** Junk.com.au says photos help but has no upload; Easy uses SMS; Demo Bros and 1800-GOT-JUNK? have nothing. No one offers "upload 2–3 photos in the form, price back by [time]".
5. **Renovation/building-site waste for homeowners mid-reno.** Pages split into "builders" cards; no one addresses the homeowner whose tiler or kitchen fitter left a pile, or sorting (clean fill vs mixed C&D) affecting price.
6. **Dismantle + remove in one visit.** Only Demo Bros mentions decks/sheds; garage/shed clear-outs that need dismantling are otherwise unaddressed.
7. **Proof of lawful disposal.** Only Junk.com.au gives an EPA number. No one offers a tip docket on request, which Quote Yard tells buyers to ask for and which builders care about.
8. **Consistency.** Three of four pages have contradictory numbers (reviews, hours, SMS numbers, award years, title vs H1). A page that is simply internally consistent stands out.

### Patterns worth adopting (in our own words, improved)
- **Price table with units:** keep the Demo Bros tier idea but add what each tier holds ("about X m³ / about a box trailer") and a separate heavy-materials line ("bricks, concrete, soil and tiles are priced by weight because the tip charges by the tonne"). Only publish figures Metro confirms (fact sheet: rubbish prices are copied from Demo Bros, TO CONFIRM).

### Mistakes to avoid
- Title tag or meta that doesn't match the ad and H1 (Demo Bros "Demolitions & Strip Outs from $800").
- "From $99" that is really per m³ with a 2 m³ minimum in fine print (Easy).
- Contradictory counts, hours, numbers or award years (Easy, 1800-GOT-JUNK?, Demo Bros).
- Unverifiable superlatives: "#1 rated", "World's largest", "Top 3", "biggest crew roster".
- Recycling percentages without a source ("up to 98%", "aims to divert 70%"). Fact sheet: every Metro proof point is unconfirmed, so no numbers until confirmed.
- Burying the phone (Junk.com.au) or forcing a 7-field form above the fold (Easy).

- Copying Demo Bros' $20M insurance, 4.9 rating or "since 2013" (fact sheet flags these as Demo Bros' numbers, not Metro's).
